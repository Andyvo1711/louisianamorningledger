Construction crews broke ground this month on a new stretch of green space along the Mississippi River in downtown Baton Rouge, capping years of planning meetings, design revisions, and public comment sessions. The project, which will convert an underused industrial lot near the river into walking paths, shaded seating areas, and a small amphitheater, is expected to take roughly eighteen months to complete.

Residents who gathered for the groundbreaking on a chilly January morning said the moment felt overdue. The site has sat mostly fenced off and unused for years, a gap in what is otherwise a growing network of riverfront attractions in downtown Baton Rouge, including the existing riverfront plaza and nearby museums.

## Filling a Gap Between Downtown and the River

For years, residents on the east side of downtown have complained that the riverfront, while scenic, was difficult to reach on foot from many neighborhoods. The new park is designed specifically to close that gap, with a pedestrian bridge over a service road and improved lighting along the connecting walkway meant to make evening visits safer and more inviting.

"I've lived here my whole life, and this piece of the river has basically been off-limits my entire adulthood," said Marcus Deshotel, who attended the groundbreaking with his two young daughters. "Having somewhere green and walkable that actually connects to the rest of downtown is going to change how people use this part of the city."

Landscape architects working on the project say they designed the space with flexibility in mind, incorporating a lawn area large enough to host food truck gatherings and small festivals alongside quieter garden sections planted with native Louisiana species meant to require less irrigation and maintenance over time. A shaded amphitheater built into a gentle slope near the water is intended to host free community concerts and outdoor movie nights once the park opens.

## Balancing Flood Resilience With Public Access

Because the site sits close to the river, engineers have had to design around the reality of seasonal water level changes and the possibility of flooding. Portions of the park's lower sections are being built with permeable surfaces and native plantings selected for their ability to tolerate periodic inundation, an approach city planners say reflects lessons learned from other riverfront projects along the Gulf Coast.

The project has not been without its critics. Some nearby business owners initially raised concerns about construction noise and temporary road closures affecting foot traffic during the build. City officials have responded by phasing construction to keep at least one pedestrian route open at all times and by scheduling the noisiest work outside of peak lunch and evening hours.

Funding for the park combines parish capital improvement dollars with a state recreational trails grant and a smaller contribution from a coalition of downtown businesses hoping to boost foot traffic. Organizers say the mixed funding model reflects a broader shift in how Baton Rouge is approaching public space investment, leaning on partnerships rather than relying solely on one funding source.

When finished, the park is expected to link seamlessly with existing riverfront paths, creating a continuous walkable stretch along the water that planners have envisioned for more than a decade. For residents who watched the fenced-off lot sit idle for years, the sight of construction equipment finally moving dirt was reason enough to celebrate, even with more than a year of work still ahead.
