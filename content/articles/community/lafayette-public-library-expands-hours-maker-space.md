The main branch of the Lafayette Public Library has quietly become one of the busiest public spaces in Acadiana this spring, thanks to extended weekend hours and a new maker space that opened on the building's second floor. On a recent Saturday afternoon, every table in the space was occupied, with children building circuits out of felt and copper tape while a few teenagers worked a 3D printer that hummed steadily in the corner.

The expansion comes after years of requests from parents and educators who said the library's previous Saturday closing time left few options for families who work during the week. Starting this year, the main branch now stays open until early evening on Saturdays, a change library staff say has already boosted weekend visits significantly.

## More Than Just Books

The maker space itself represents a shift in how the library system thinks about its role in the community. Alongside the usual shelves of fiction and reference materials, the new room includes sewing machines, a laser cutter reserved for supervised teen and adult sessions, and a bank of computers loaded with basic design software. Library staff run free walk-in workshops several times a week, covering everything from beginner coding to hand embroidery.

"A lot of people still think of the library as just a place to check out a novel," said Danielle Broussard, a Lafayette parent whose two children attend the Saturday robotics sessions. "Now it's the one place in town where my kids can build something with their hands for free, rain or shine."

The push for hands-on programming reflects broader patterns seen at library systems across the state, but Lafayette's version leans heavily into the region's do-it-yourself and craft traditions, with quilting circles and traditional Cajun music instrument-building workshops sharing space with newer digital tools. Local musicians have volunteered to lead sessions teaching teens how to build simple stringed instruments, tying the maker space back to Acadiana's cultural roots.

## Branch Libraries Feel the Ripple Effect

The changes at the main branch have prompted renewed attention to Lafayette's smaller neighborhood branches as well. Several of the system's satellite locations, some of which have not seen major renovations in over a decade, are now under review for their own updates, including possible smaller-scale maker corners geared toward younger children.

Library administrators say funding for the expansion came through a mix of parish budget allocations and a state grant aimed at modernizing public facilities outside of New Orleans and Baton Rouge, the state's two largest media and cultural hubs. Officials hope the investment signals that smaller regional systems can offer the same kind of modern programming typically associated with bigger-city libraries.

Not every change has been welcomed without debate. Some longtime patrons have raised concerns about noise levels bleeding from the maker space into quieter reading areas, prompting staff to install soundproofing panels between the two zones. Others have asked for expanded hours to extend into weekday evenings as well, a request library leadership says they are studying for the coming budget cycle.

For now, the Saturday crowds suggest the investment is paying off. Staff report that card sign-ups have increased noticeably since the new hours and maker space launched, with many of the new patrons being families with school-age children who previously visited only occasionally. As one library aide put it while restocking a shelf of laser-cut wooden puzzles, the building finally feels like it is being used the way a modern community library should be.
