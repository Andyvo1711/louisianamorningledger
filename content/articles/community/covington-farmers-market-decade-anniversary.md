Early on Saturday mornings, well before most of Covington has finished its coffee, vendors begin setting up canopy tents and folding tables along the market grounds near downtown, unloading crates of tomatoes, baskets of blackberries, and coolers packed with fresh eggs. This summer marks ten years since the Covington Farmers Market first opened with a handful of growers, and what began as a modest weekly gathering has grown into one of the north shore's most reliable community traditions.

The market's early days were humble by comparison to today's bustling Saturday crowds. Organizers recall starting with fewer than ten vendors and uncertain foot traffic, relying largely on word of mouth among gardening enthusiasts and a small core of regulars to keep the market afloat through its first uncertain seasons.

## From Folding Tables to a Weekly Institution

Today, the market regularly hosts several dozen vendors selling everything from seasonal produce and pasture-raised meats to handmade soaps and baked goods, drawing steady crowds of shoppers from Covington and surrounding St. Tammany Parish communities. Musicians often perform near the market entrance, and a rotating cast of food vendors has turned market mornings into something closer to a neighborhood gathering than a simple shopping errand.

"I remember when it was just me and maybe five other farmers hoping somebody would show up," said Otis Ferrand, a longtime area grower who has sold produce at the market since its opening season. "Now I sell out of tomatoes most Saturdays before ten o'clock. It's not just a market anymore. It's where half the town catches up on their week."

That community function has become as central to the market's identity as the produce itself. Regular shoppers describe the Saturday routine as much a social occasion as a grocery trip, with familiar vendors greeting returning customers by name and neighbors catching up over coffee from a rotating lineup of local roasters.

## Supporting Small Growers Through Changing Seasons

Beyond its role as a community gathering spot, the market has become an important economic outlet for small-scale farmers throughout the region, many of whom rely on direct sales to consumers to make their operations financially viable in ways that selling through larger distributors often cannot match. Market organizers have worked over the years to keep vendor fees manageable, particularly for smaller growers just starting out, while building relationships with local restaurants that now source seasonal ingredients directly from market vendors.

The market has weathered its share of challenges over the past decade, including severe weather that occasionally forced cancellations and periods when vendor turnout dipped during difficult growing seasons. Organizers credit a loyal base of regular shoppers with helping the market recover from these setbacks and continue growing steadily year over year.

To mark the ten-year milestone, organizers are planning an expanded anniversary market later this summer featuring additional vendors, live music throughout the morning, and a small exhibit chronicling the market's growth through photographs contributed by longtime vendors and shoppers. Several founding vendors who have sold at the market since its first season plan to attend, a testament to the loyalty the market has built on both sides of the table.

As the market looks toward its second decade, organizers say the goal remains largely unchanged from its founding mission: giving small growers a reliable place to sell what they raise, and giving the Covington community a weekly reason to gather, one Saturday morning at a time.
