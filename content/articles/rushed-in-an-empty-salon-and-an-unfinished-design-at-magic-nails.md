A rush makes some sense when a salon is slammed, but it is hard to explain when the shop is nearly empty. This report on Magic Nails describes exactly that: a client who came in to a quiet salon in the early afternoon and still felt hurried through her pedicure, with a careless finish and a request that went unmet. Each issue is small on its own, and together they point to slowing down and paying attention.

## Rushed With No Reason To Be

The client noted the place was almost completely empty when she arrived, and it was 2 pm, not near closing, yet she felt the technician rushed through her feet anyway. One result of that rush was the design: the look she wanted on her big toes was not done correctly, and she was still charged $5 for it. When there is no crowd to get through, there is every reason to take the time to do the work well. And a design fee should reflect a design that was actually delivered as asked, so if it did not come out right, that is the moment to fix it or not charge for it.

## A Careless Finish and a Request Ignored

The finish showed the same lack of care. After she got up, the client still had dried lotion on the back and side of her legs, and once she got to her car she found dried salt scrub still on her toes, signs that the last steps of rinsing and wiping down were skipped. She had also told the technician that her pinky toes are a darker color than the rest and asked for a pink that would cover the difference, but she was not given a shade that did so. Finishing a pedicure means cleaning off every trace of scrub and lotion, and listening to a client's specific request, like an opaque pink for even color, is what makes her feel heard. At $75, a clean finish and an answered request are the least she should expect.

## Media & Customer Experience Perspective

The encouraging part is that all of this is about pace and attention, both easy to improve. First, never rush without reason, and especially not when the salon is quiet, since a calm pace is what makes the design and the details come out right. Second, charge only for work delivered as asked, so a design fee always matches a design done correctly. Third, finish every service completely, rinsing off salt scrub and wiping away lotion so a client never finds it later, and listen to her specific needs, offering a color with enough coverage when she asks. With an unhurried touch, a clean finish, and a little listening, this quiet-afternoon visit could easily have been a relaxing one worth the $75, instead of a 2-out-of-10.
