The University of New Orleans has long held a distinction few universities in the country can claim: one of only a handful of accredited naval architecture and marine engineering programs in the United States. This year, the program is expanding its course offerings and lab capacity, reflecting steady demand from Gulf Coast shipbuilders and the maritime industry that anchors much of the region's industrial economy.

New Orleans sits at the base of the Mississippi River, one of the busiest inland waterway systems in the world, and the surrounding region is home to shipyards, offshore energy companies, and port operations that together employ thousands of workers with specialized maritime engineering skills. UNO's program has historically served as one of the primary local sources of talent for that industry, producing engineers who go on to design everything from towboats to offshore support vessels.

## Growing With the Industry

Program administrators say the expansion includes new computational tools for hull design and structural analysis, giving students access to the same industry-standard software used by shipbuilding firms across the Gulf Coast. Enrollment in the program has grown steadily in recent years, a trend faculty attribute to renewed investment in domestic shipbuilding and offshore wind development, both of which require engineers trained in marine-specific structural and hydrodynamic principles that differ meaningfully from general mechanical engineering coursework.

Students in the program complete coursework in ship design, marine structures, and hydrodynamics, along with hands-on projects that often involve designing vessels to meet specific operational requirements, such as reduced fuel consumption or improved stability in rough water. Many students also complete internships with shipyards and engineering firms located within a short drive of the New Orleans campus, giving them direct exposure to the industry before graduation.

## A Pipeline for the Port and Beyond

The Port of New Orleans and related maritime logistics operations represent another significant source of career opportunities for UNO graduates, even for those who don't go directly into vessel design. Program leaders note that the analytical and technical skills developed through naval architecture coursework translate well into broader maritime engineering and logistics roles, giving graduates flexibility across the industry.

University officials describe the program's growth as part of a deliberate strategy to strengthen ties between the university and the maritime employers who depend on a steady supply of specialized engineering talent. Representatives from regional shipbuilding companies have participated in curriculum advisory sessions, offering input on which technical skills are most valuable in current hiring, from proficiency in specific design software to familiarity with updated Coast Guard safety regulations.

The expansion also includes new scholarship opportunities aimed at attracting students from across Louisiana who might not otherwise consider a specialized engineering discipline, particularly students from coastal parishes with existing ties to the fishing, shipping, or offshore energy industries. Faculty say students with firsthand familiarity with boats and waterways often bring valuable intuition to naval architecture coursework, even before they have formal engineering training.

As global shipping patterns shift and interest in offshore wind energy grows along the Gulf Coast, UNO's naval architecture program finds itself positioned at the intersection of a traditional regional industry and an emerging one. University leaders say they expect demand for graduates to remain strong for years to come, reinforcing New Orleans' role as a hub for maritime engineering education on the Gulf Coast.
