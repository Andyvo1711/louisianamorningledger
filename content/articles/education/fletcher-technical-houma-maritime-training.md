Fletcher Technical Community College in Houma is expanding its maritime and offshore training programs, adding new vessel simulation equipment and course sections to prepare workers for careers in the region's marine transportation and offshore energy industries. The expansion reflects Houma's enduring role as a center for maritime commerce along the Gulf Coast, where offshore supply vessels, towing companies, and marine construction firms depend on a steady supply of trained mariners and technicians.

Terrebonne Parish and the surrounding bayou region have deep historical ties to the maritime and fishing industries, and Fletcher's programs are designed to build directly on that regional identity while adapting to the modern technical demands of contemporary vessel operations. Students in the maritime program work toward credentials recognized by the United States Coast Guard, preparing them for entry-level positions aboard offshore supply vessels and other commercial marine operations based out of the Port of Terrebonne and nearby facilities.

## Simulators Bring the Water to Campus

The expanded program includes new bridge simulation technology that allows students to practice vessel navigation and maneuvering in a range of simulated weather and traffic conditions before ever operating an actual vessel. This kind of simulation-based training has become increasingly standard across maritime education programs nationally, offering a way for students to build practical experience and confidence in scenarios that would be too risky or logistically difficult to replicate during early stages of hands-on training.

College officials say the investment in simulation technology also allows the program to train more students simultaneously than traditional on-the-water instruction alone would permit, addressing capacity constraints that had previously limited how many students the program could accommodate each semester. Instructors, many of whom bring years of firsthand offshore experience to the classroom, use the simulators to walk students through both routine operations and emergency response scenarios.

## Serving an Industry With Deep Local Roots

Fletcher's maritime training programs maintain close relationships with regional employers, including offshore supply vessel operators and marine construction companies headquartered in and around Houma. These employer relationships help ensure that program curriculum stays current with evolving industry certification requirements and equipment standards, while also creating direct pathways for students to secure employment shortly after completing their coursework.

Beyond vessel operation training, the college has expanded course offerings in marine diesel technology, addressing strong regional demand for technicians capable of maintaining and repairing the engines that power the area's extensive commercial fleet. Program administrators describe this technical track as complementary to the navigation-focused curriculum, giving students multiple entry points into the maritime workforce depending on their individual interests and aptitudes.

The expansion also includes new outreach efforts aimed at local high school students throughout the Houma area, introducing younger students to maritime career pathways earlier in their education through campus tours and short introductory workshops. College officials say this early exposure is particularly important in a region where maritime careers have historically been passed down informally through family connections to the industry, and where formal educational pathways can help broaden access to students without existing family ties to marine work.

As offshore energy activity in the Gulf of Mexico continues to evolve, including growing interest in offshore wind alongside traditional oil and gas operations, Fletcher's maritime program aims to keep its graduates prepared for a workforce that remains central to the economic identity of Houma and the surrounding bayou communities.
