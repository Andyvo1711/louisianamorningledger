Ten years ago, finding a decent cup of coffee in Covington after nine in the morning meant settling for whatever was left in a gas station carafe. These days, the northshore town has quietly become one of the more surprising coffee destinations in the region, with a cluster of independent shops drawing steady crowds from across St. Tammany Parish and beyond.

At the center of that shift sits Cypress & Chicory, a converted shotgun house just off the roundabout downtown, where a roaster the size of a washing machine runs most mornings, filling the block with the smell of beans turning from green to a deep, oily brown.

## From Sleepy Town to Coffee Destination

"When we opened, people kept asking if Covington could really support a shop that only did pour-overs and single-origin beans," said the shop's founder, who left a corporate job in New Orleans to open the roastery five years ago. "Turns out the town was ready. It just didn't have anywhere to prove it yet."

Cypress & Chicory's success helped pave the way for a handful of similar spots to open nearby, including a bicycle-themed café near the Bogue Falaya river trail and a bakery-café hybrid tucked into a renovated garage a few blocks from the historic trailhead downtown. Rather than competing directly, the shops have developed a loose, friendly rhythm, with regulars often making a loop between two or three spots over the course of a single morning.

Chicory itself, the ground root long blended into coffee across south Louisiana as a Depression-era stretching technique turned beloved flavor tradition, has become something of a signature touch for several of the new shops, offered as an optional addition rather than the default the way it traditionally was served at old-line New Orleans coffeehouses.

## A Gathering Place for a Changing Town

What's brought the biggest changes to Covington's daily rhythm isn't just the coffee itself but what's grown up around it. Remote workers who once needed to commute into New Orleans for meetings now hold them from a corner table at Cypress & Chicory, laptop open, oat milk latte within reach. Retirees who moved to the northshore for its quieter pace have found in the new coffee shops something they didn't expect: a reliable place to run into the same familiar faces every morning.

A watercolor painter who sets up most mornings near the shop's front window said the coffee scene has changed who she sees on a given day almost as much as it's changed what she drinks. "I used to paint at home, alone, with a pot of drip coffee I made myself," she said. "Now I paint here, and half the town wanders through in a two-hour stretch. It's better company than I expected coffee to bring."

The shops have also become informal community bulletin boards, their corkboards and front windows layered with flyers for art markets, local music nights, and volunteer cleanups along the Tchefuncte River. A monthly open mic night hosted jointly by two of the shops now regularly fills their combined seating, spilling customers out onto the sidewalk with cups in hand.

For a town that spent decades in the shadow of New Orleans' better-known food and drink culture, Covington's coffee renaissance has given it something distinctly its own: a set of daily rituals, brewed strong and dark, chicory optional, that have turned quiet corners of downtown into some of the busiest social hubs on the northshore.
