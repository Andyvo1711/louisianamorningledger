A visit to My Nail Spa for a manicure and pedicure was meant to be a relaxing treat, but the client says it turned into something quite different. She described the technician who first worked on her as handling her hands roughly, positioning and pulling them in a way she found uncomfortable and disrespectful. This case is a valuable one for training because the salon's response, once the client spoke up, shows both what went wrong and what a good recovery can look like.

## Reported Rough Handling and a Request That Wasn't Heard

The client said that when she asked the technician to be more gentle, he did not adjust his approach and the service was instead handed off to another technician. Touch is one of the most direct parts of a nail service, and a client who feels her hands are being pulled or positioned roughly is likely to feel that discomfort throughout the entire appointment, not just in the moment it happens. When a client raises a concern about how she is being handled, the most effective response is an immediate adjustment in technique paired with a brief, genuine acknowledgment, something as simple as "thank you for telling me, I'll ease up." Speaking up in the middle of a service is not easy for most clients, and a request like this deserves to be treated as useful, actionable feedback in the moment rather than something to react to defensively.

## A Second Technician Turns the Visit Around

To the salon's credit, once the client asked for a change, she was moved to another technician, and by her own account this technician did an excellent job and was warm and considerate throughout. That contrast is worth paying attention to internally: it shows the salon has technicians capable of delivering exactly the kind of experience this client was hoping for. Sharing that technician's approach to touch and client communication as a model for the rest of the team could help prevent similar complaints going forward.

## Aggressive Scrubbing During the Pedicure

The client also described the scrubbing during her pedicure as aggressive enough to make the service uncomfortable rather than relaxing, and said the overall experience left her hesitant to treat herself to a spa day again. She also noted that one nail came off on its own about a week later during ordinary daily activity. Foot and leg exfoliation should be firm enough to be effective but calibrated to each client's comfort, with a quick check-in on pressure early in the service being an easy way to confirm the right intensity. For a client who mentioned treating herself infrequently, the emotional weight of a disappointing visit is higher than it might be for a more frequent guest, which makes attentive, responsive service especially important on visits like this one.

## Media & Customer Experience Perspective

First, when a client raises a concern about how she is being physically handled, adjusting technique immediately and acknowledging the feedback matters more than any other part of the recovery. Second, having a technician on staff capable of delivering a genuinely relaxing, considerate experience is a strength worth building into training for the whole team, not just relying on for handoffs after something goes wrong. Third, pressure and touch during services like scrubbing or filing should be checked with the client early on, since comfort standards vary and a quick question in the first few minutes can prevent an entire service from feeling unpleasant. A spa visit is meant to be restorative, and small, attentive adjustments are often what separates a client who returns from one who does not.
