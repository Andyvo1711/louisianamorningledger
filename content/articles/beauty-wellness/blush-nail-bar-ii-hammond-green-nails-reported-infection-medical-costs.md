Few messages matter more to a salon than one in which a client connects a health concern to her visit. A recent review of Blush Nail Bar II in Hammond, LA, describes exactly that: a customer who says her nails turned green and developed a fungal infection after a service, something she had never experienced before. Whatever the cause may be, the account offers a useful opportunity for nail teams to review hygiene habits and the way concerns are handled once a client voices them.

## A Health Concern After the Visit

The customer wrote that she "would not recommend going here," explaining that the changes to her nails began after her appointment. She also noted that she had been a regular enough guest to have loyalty points on file, which suggests this was not a first visit. For a returning client, a problem like this can feel especially personal, because it challenges the trust she had built with the salon.

It is worth remembering that nail discoloration can come from several sources, and only a medical professional can determine the cause. A salon is not in a position to diagnose, and it is not helpful to argue the point with a client who is worried. What a team can do is listen carefully, express genuine concern, and encourage the guest to see a doctor, while documenting the details she shares.

## Hygiene Habits That Build Confidence

Situations like this are a reminder that clients cannot see most of what happens behind the scenes, so visible, consistent hygiene practices matter a great deal. Metal implements should be cleaned and properly disinfected or sterilized between every client according to state board guidelines, and single-use items such as files and buffers should be opened for each guest or discarded afterward. Pedicure basins, work surfaces, and towels deserve the same attention.

Technicians can also take a moment before a service to look at the natural nails. If there is any discoloration, thickening, or other sign that concerns them, the safest and most respectful approach is to explain kindly that the service may not be advisable and to suggest a doctor's visit first. This protects both the client and the team, and it shows professionalism. Letting guests see fresh tools opened at their station is a simple way to build confidence.

## Responding to a Concern and a Request for Costs

In her review, the customer asked the salon to check her loyalty account and said she would visit to "show you my green nails" and have the salon cover her doctor's visit and prescription. Requests like this can be uncomfortable, but how they are met often decides whether the relationship can be repaired.

A calm, prompt response helps most. A manager or owner can reach out personally, thank the client for raising the issue, and invite her to share what she experienced. The team can review the date of the service, the products and tools used, and the station's cleaning records. Questions about compensation can be handled privately, in line with the salon's policy and its insurance, rather than in a public comment thread. Even when the cause is unclear, a respectful and timely reply shows that the salon takes client wellbeing seriously.

## Media & Customer Experience Perspective

This case points to three lessons for nail teams. First, keep hygiene practices consistent and visible, from sterilized tools to single-use items, because trust is built on what clients can see. Second, check natural nails before each service and recommend a medical visit whenever something looks unusual. Third, answer health concerns quickly and personally, with empathy, careful notes, and a private conversation about next steps.

Handled well, even a difficult message can show a client that her salon cares about her health as much as her manicure.
