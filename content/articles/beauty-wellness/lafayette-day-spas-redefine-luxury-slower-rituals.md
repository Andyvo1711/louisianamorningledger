For years, the standard day spa menu grew longer every season: more add-ons, more upgrades, more choices to scroll through before a guest even booked. In Lafayette, that trend has started to reverse. A handful of local spas are cutting their menus down, betting that clients would rather have fewer options done exceptionally well than a wall of upsells.

At Cypress & Stone Spa near the Oil Center, the treatment list now fits on a single page. Owner Renee Deshotel says that was intentional. "We used to have four kinds of facials and six kinds of massage, and half of our front desk time was spent explaining the differences," she said. "Now we have one excellent facial and one excellent massage, each ninety minutes, and people leave actually relaxed instead of rushed."

## Fewer Choices, Longer Appointments

The shift reflects a broader rethinking of what luxury means in a spa setting. Rather than packing a visit with extras, Lafayette spas are lengthening the core appointment itself, building in extra time for a proper consultation, a slow warm-up period, and a quiet cool-down before the guest ever leaves the treatment room.

Deshotel said the change came after listening to years of client feedback that pointed in the same direction: people did not want more services, they wanted more time. A ninety-minute massage that begins with ten unhurried minutes of breathing and warm towels, she said, feels entirely different from a rushed sixty-minute session squeezed between two other bookings.

That philosophy extends to the physical space as well. Several Lafayette spas have removed televisions from waiting areas, dimmed lighting throughout common spaces, and swapped background music for quieter ambient soundscapes recorded specifically for their locations. The goal, staff say, is to start the relaxation process well before a guest reaches the treatment table.

## Local Ingredients, Local Identity

Lafayette's spa scene has also leaned into regional identity, incorporating ingredients associated with south Louisiana into scrubs, wraps, and facial treatments. Rice bran, cane sugar, and locally sourced honey show up frequently on updated menus, framed not as a marketing gimmick but as a genuine point of pride.

"We wanted our spa to feel like it could only exist here," Deshotel said. "Not a generic spa that happens to be in Lafayette, but something that actually reflects Acadiana."

Staff training has shifted accordingly. New estheticians and massage therapists at Cypress & Stone go through an onboarding period that includes education on the region's climate and how it affects skin, since humidity and heat create different needs than the dry-climate spa traditions many treatment protocols were originally built around.

## Booking Patterns Are Changing Too

The slower approach appears to be resonating. Deshotel said average appointment length has grown by nearly forty percent over the past two years, even as the total number of services offered has shrunk. Repeat bookings have also increased, with many clients now scheduling standing monthly appointments rather than visiting only for special occasions.

Other Lafayette spas have noticed the same pattern and are adjusting their own offerings to match, moving away from long menus stuffed with quick add-ons in favor of a smaller number of signature treatments built around unhurried time.

For Deshotel, the lesson has been straightforward. "People aren't paying for a longer list," she said. "They're paying to feel like, for ninety minutes, nobody needs anything from them."
