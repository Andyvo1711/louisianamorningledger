Tucked into a converted cottage just off the trace in Covington, Pine & Palm Bodywork does not have a juice bar, a robe closet, or a rack of scented candles for sale. Owner and licensed massage therapist Daniel Guillory built the studio around a simpler idea: that a massage practice focused purely on therapeutic outcomes could stand on its own, without the usual spa-day trappings layered on top.

"People sometimes walk in expecting a whole experience with music and mood lighting and a gift shop," Guillory said. "What we actually offer is closer to physical therapy than pampering. It's quiet, it's focused, and it's built around what your body actually needs that day."

## Bodywork With a Clinical Edge

Guillory's background includes work with local sports teams and rehabilitation clinics before he opened his own studio two years ago, and that clinical grounding shows in how sessions are structured. Every new client fills out a detailed intake covering injury history, activity level, and specific areas of tension before ever getting on the table, and therapists adjust technique accordingly, blending deep tissue work, myofascial release, and trigger point therapy based on what the assessment reveals.

The studio has become a regular stop for runners training for the Northshore's growing calendar of road races, as well as for people managing chronic tension from desk jobs or old injuries. Guillory said roughly a third of his client base now consists of standing weekly or biweekly appointments, patients who treat massage as ongoing maintenance rather than an occasional treat.

"We're not trying to be everything to everyone," he said. "If someone wants a relaxing spa massage with essential oils and a warm blanket, that's a completely valid thing to want, and there are wonderful places for that. We built something different."

## Training Therapists to Listen First

Part of what sets Pine & Palm apart, according to Guillory, is how much time therapists spend talking with clients before and during sessions. Rather than defaulting to a set sequence of techniques, therapists are trained to check in throughout the appointment, adjusting pressure and focus based on real-time feedback.

"A lot of massage therapy gets rushed because the therapist is following a script instead of following the person on the table," Guillory said. "We tell our team: the plan is a starting point, not a rulebook. If something's not working for that person that day, change it."

New therapists joining the studio go through an extended onboarding period that includes shadowing sessions and case review discussions, an unusual step for a small independent practice but one Guillory considers essential to maintaining consistency across the team.

## A Growing Northshore Demand

Interest in recovery-focused bodywork has grown noticeably across the Northshore in recent years, driven partly by an increasingly active population of runners, cyclists, and weekend athletes drawn to the area's trails and open roads. Guillory said he has fielded steady inquiries from people relocating from larger cities who are accustomed to more clinically minded massage practices and were surprised, and pleased, to find one in Covington.

He has resisted expanding too quickly, preferring to keep the studio small enough that he can personally train every new hire. "I'd rather stay this size and have people trust exactly what they're going to get," he said, "than grow fast and lose the thing that makes it work."

For now, that thing seems to be working. Pine & Palm's appointment book regularly fills two to three weeks out, a steady demand Guillory attributes less to marketing than to word of mouth among clients who say the sessions genuinely helped.
