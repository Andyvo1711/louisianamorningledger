The line of stainless steel cold plunge tubs along the back wall of Driftwood Recovery Lab looks more at home in a professional sports facility than a strip of downtown Lake Charles storefronts. That contrast is precisely what founder Ashley Comeaux was counting on when she opened the studio last fall, betting that recovery-focused wellness, long popular in larger cities, was overdue for a local foothold.

"People here were already driving to Houston or flying somewhere to try this stuff," Comeaux said. "I figured, why not just bring it here instead of watching everyone else spend money and gas getting it elsewhere."

## What Recovery Actually Means

Driftwood's offerings center on three main services: cold plunge immersion, infrared sauna sessions, and compression therapy using pneumatic boots that rhythmically squeeze the legs to promote circulation. Comeaux said the studio deliberately avoids framing itself as a spa, positioning the space instead as a performance and recovery facility aimed at athletes, laborers with physically demanding jobs, and anyone dealing with chronic inflammation or slow recovery from exercise.

Sessions typically follow a contrast protocol, alternating between the sauna's dry heat and the plunge tubs' near-freezing water, a combination Comeaux said produces the studio's most requested and most talked-about experience. New clients are walked through a safety briefing before their first cold plunge, including guidance on breathing technique and how long to stay submerged based on individual tolerance and health history.

"The first thirty seconds in that tub humbles everybody," Comeaux said with a laugh. "But almost everyone comes out saying they feel more clear-headed than they expected."

## Building Trust With a Skeptical Market

Introducing an unfamiliar wellness concept to a market accustomed to traditional gyms and spas required a deliberate education effort, according to Comeaux. Driftwood hosts free monthly information sessions where prospective clients can ask questions and try a shortened version of each service before committing to a membership, a strategy Comeaux credits with converting a significant share of skeptics into regulars.

The studio has also built relationships with local physical therapy clinics and a few area sports coaches, who refer clients dealing with soreness, minor injuries, or general recovery needs. Comeaux said those referral relationships have become one of the studio's most reliable sources of new membership, lending the space a degree of credibility that marketing alone could not have achieved as quickly.

## Staffing for Safety First

Because cold plunge and heat therapy carry real physiological considerations, Comeaux has been selective about staffing, requiring all recovery attendants to complete certification in both modalities before working independently with clients. Staff are trained to screen for contraindications such as certain heart conditions or pregnancy, and to monitor clients throughout sessions rather than simply setting a timer and stepping away.

"This isn't something you can just wing," she said. "We take the safety training seriously because the stakes, while usually low, are real."

## A Concept Finding Its Footing

Six months in, Comeaux said Driftwood's membership base has grown steadily beyond her initial projections, with a mix of regular athletes, shift workers on their feet all day, and curious newcomers who saw the studio on their way through downtown. She has begun exploring a second cold plunge tub to manage demand during the studio's busiest evening hours.

"Lake Charles has always had people who wanted this kind of thing," she said. "They just didn't have anywhere close to home to actually do it. Now they do."
