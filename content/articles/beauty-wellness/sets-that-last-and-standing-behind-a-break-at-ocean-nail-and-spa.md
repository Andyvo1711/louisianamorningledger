A client who returns again and again, even after being let down, is giving a salon the most valuable thing there is: another chance. This report on Ocean Nail & Spa describes a customer who did exactly that, and what finally led her to share her experience. Both issues she raised, sets that did not last and being charged to fix them, are core to how a salon earns lasting trust.

## Nails That Kept Breaking Too Soon

By her own account, the client gave the salon several opportunities, coming back despite being unsatisfied each time because her nails kept breaking in less than a week. On her most recent visit, two nails broke within just a couple of days. A set is meant to hold up to normal daily life, and repeated early breakage usually points to something fixable in the process, such as prep, product choice, application thickness, or how the free edge is sealed. When nails break this quickly and this often, it is worth a careful look at technique, because durability is a large part of what the client is paying for.

## Charged to Fix a Set That Failed

The part that finally tipped the balance was the charge. She had paid $55 for the set, and when she returned a couple of days later because two nails had broken, she was charged an extra $20. She had also asked for a color change on that visit, which is a reasonable thing to price on its own, but the result was that fixing a set that failed almost immediately felt like it came with a bill. When work breaks down within days, the repair itself should be covered by the salon, no questions asked. If a client separately requests something new, like a color change, the fair approach is to make the breakage fix free and clearly explain any charge for the new request, so it never feels like she is paying to correct the salon's own work.

## Media & Customer Experience Perspective

The encouraging part is that both of these are very fixable, and getting them right is what keeps a patient customer for good. First, build for durability: solid prep, the right product for the client's lifestyle, even application, and a well-sealed edge go a long way toward a set that lasts its full life instead of breaking in days. A quick conversation about how a client uses her hands helps match the service to real life. Second, stand behind the work with a simple, stated policy: if a nail breaks within a set number of days, the fix is free. And when a client also asks for something new on the same visit, separate the two clearly, so the repair stays complimentary and any new charge is transparent and agreed in advance. This client showed real loyalty by coming back so many times. Sets that held and a free, gracious fix would have rewarded that patience and turned her into a lasting regular rather than a lost one.
