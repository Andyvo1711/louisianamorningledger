Wedding planning in Alexandria has always run on its own timeline, but bridal beauty artist Delphine Ardoin says the calendar has shifted noticeably in the last two years. Where trial sessions for hair and makeup once typically happened a month or two before a wedding, Ardoin now regularly books trials nine to twelve months in advance, often before a bride has finalized her dress.

"I used to have a pretty predictable rhythm to my year," said Ardoin, who runs Camellia Bridal Beauty out of a studio near downtown Alexandria. "Now brides are reaching out the week after they get engaged, trying to lock in a trial date before I fill up. It's changed how I have to plan my whole calendar."

## Why the Timeline Moved Up

Ardoin attributes much of the shift to increased competition for popular wedding dates and venues in Central Louisiana, which has pushed brides to lock in every vendor, including beauty services, as early as possible to avoid losing a preferred artist to another booking. Social media has played a role as well, with brides increasingly arriving at consultations having already researched specific looks and wanting extensive lead time to test and refine them.

"A trial isn't just 'let's see how this looks' anymore," Ardoin said. "Brides come in with three or four reference photos, a very specific idea of their skin tone goals, sometimes questions about how a look will hold up through humidity and tears both. It's a much more involved conversation than it used to be."

That added complexity has pushed Ardoin to lengthen her trial appointments considerably, from what used to be a ninety-minute session to a full two-and-a-half-hour block that includes time for photographs in different lighting conditions, since brides increasingly want to see how a finished look reads in both natural daylight and the dimmer lighting typical of evening reception venues.

## Planning Around Louisiana's Climate

Humidity remains one of the most consistent concerns Ardoin hears from clients, particularly for weddings scheduled during the warmer months. She has developed a standard humidity-resistant approach for both hair and makeup, favoring long-wear setting techniques and styles less prone to falling flat in thick, wet air, and she recommends most spring and summer brides schedule at least part of their trial outdoors to genuinely test how a look holds up.

"There's no point doing a trial in an air-conditioned studio if the wedding is an outdoor June ceremony," she said. "I want to see what actually happens to that hairstyle after twenty minutes outside in July."

## A Ripple Effect Across the Bridal Party

The earlier booking trend has extended beyond brides themselves. Ardoin said she has seen a marked increase in bridal party members, mothers, and even close friends booking their own trial sessions well ahead of the wedding date, wanting to coordinate looks and ensure availability on the actual wedding day.

To manage the increased demand, Ardoin has begun training a small team of associate artists under her direct supervision, allowing Camellia Bridal Beauty to take on larger wedding parties without sacrificing the personal attention she considers central to her reputation.

"I still do every bride's trial and wedding day look myself," she said. "But for a twelve-person bridal party, I need trained hands I trust working alongside me."

As engagement season approaches this winter, Ardoin expects the early-booking pattern to continue, a shift she has come to see less as a passing trend and more as the new normal for how Central Louisiana brides plan their big day.
