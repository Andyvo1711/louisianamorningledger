For years, clients with curly and coily hair in Baton Rouge faced a familiar problem: a great haircut in the salon chair that fell apart within a block of walking outside. Standard dry-cutting techniques, developed largely for straighter hair types, often left curls uneven once humidity took hold. That gap has fueled a wave of new curl-specialty training among Baton Rouge stylists over the past two years.

At Hollyhock Hair Collective near the LSU campus, stylist and co-owner Marcus Boudreaux has built an entire side of his book around curl work, cutting hair dry, curl by curl, rather than relying on wet-cut methods that can shrink unpredictably once hair dries in Louisiana's thick air.

"You can't cut curly hair the same way you'd cut straight hair and expect it to behave here," Boudreaux said. "Our humidity is not gentle. If you don't account for how much a curl is going to spring up and shift once it's dry, you'll hand someone a shape that only looks right for about twenty minutes."

## Dry Cutting Takes Hold

Dry-cutting techniques, where each curl is assessed and trimmed in its natural, unstretched state, have become the standard approach among Baton Rouge's growing roster of curl specialists. The method takes considerably longer than a traditional wet cut, often ninety minutes to two hours for a full shaping, but stylists say the payoff is a haircut that holds its intended silhouette regardless of the day's weather.

Boudreaux said demand has outpaced what a typical salon education prepares stylists for. Many curl specialists in the area have sought additional certification beyond standard cosmetology training, attending workshops specifically focused on curl pattern assessment, porosity testing, and product layering for different curl types.

## Product Knowledge as a Selling Point

Cutting technique is only part of the equation. Baton Rouge salons catering to curly-haired clients have also become more selective about the products they recommend, steering away from heavy silicones that can weigh curls down in humid conditions and toward lighter, water-based creams and gels that allow curls to breathe.

"We spend almost as much time on the product conversation as we do on the cut," Boudreaux said. "If someone walks out with the wrong gel for their curl type, the humidity here will undo everything we just did."

Several salons now offer curl consultations as a standalone service, separate from the haircut itself, where clients can get personalized product recommendations without committing to a full appointment. The consultations have proven popular among clients transitioning away from chemical straightening treatments and relearning how to care for their natural texture.

## Building Community Around Curl Care

Beyond the technical side, curl specialists in Baton Rouge say they are also filling an emotional gap for clients who spent years feeling underserved by salons that treated curly hair as an afterthought. Boudreaux hosts periodic curl-care workshops at Hollyhock, walking small groups of clients through at-home styling techniques, from finger-coiling to diffusing methods that minimize frizz in the area's thick summer air.

"A lot of people come in here who were told for years that their hair was hard to work with," he said. "It's not hard. It just needed someone who actually understood it and understood this climate."

As word spreads, Boudreaux said his curl-focused clientele has grown steadily, drawing appointments from as far as Denham Springs and Gonzales, evidence that specialized, climate-aware haircutting has found a real and growing audience across the Baton Rouge area.
