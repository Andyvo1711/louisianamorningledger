Marcus Whitfield spent his twenties booking bands into small clubs around Shreveport-Bossier for gas money and cover charges before deciding the region deserved something more permanent than a rotating cast of pop-up venues. In 2019, he opened the first Red River Rhythm venue, a mid-sized concert hall near the Bossier City casino district, betting that steady tourist foot traffic combined with a genuine commitment to regional musicians could sustain a real entertainment business. Today, Red River Rhythm Entertainment Group operates three venues and an artist management arm representing a dozen regional acts.

"Bossier had all this casino traffic walking past empty storefronts every night, and nowhere close by to actually hear a great local band," Whitfield said, standing in the sound booth of his flagship venue. "I figured if I could capture even a fraction of the people already out for the evening and give them somewhere worth stopping, the venue could work. It did, but barely, for a while."

## Betting on Homegrown Talent Over Touring Acts

Unlike many regional concert venues that build their booking calendars primarily around national touring acts passing through, Whitfield made a deliberate choice to dedicate a significant share of Red River Rhythm's stage time to Louisiana-based musicians, spanning zydeco, blues, country, and hip-hop acts from across the state. The approach initially drew skepticism from investors who worried the venue couldn't fill seats without recognizable national names.

"Everybody told me local-first booking was a nice mission statement but not a business model," Whitfield said. "I told them Louisiana has more homegrown musical talent per capita than almost anywhere in the country, and casino visitors from out of state are curious about it, not just here for the same touring acts they could see in Dallas or Atlanta."

That bet paid off. Red River Rhythm's flagship venue now regularly sells out weekend shows featuring regional artists, and the company's artist management arm has helped several previously local-only acts book their first tours beyond Louisiana's borders, including a zydeco group from Opelousas that Whitfield's team helped land a Texas festival circuit run in 2025.

## Expanding Bossier City's Entertainment Footprint

Whitfield opened a second, smaller venue in 2022 focused on emerging and unsigned acts, giving younger musicians a lower-stakes stage to develop before graduating to the main room, and a third venue in early 2025 combining a listening room format with a recording studio available to local artists at below-market rates.

"Marcus built the kind of ladder that just didn't exist here before," said Jolene Aucoin, a Bossier-based singer-songwriter who played her first paid show at Red River Rhythm's smaller venue before graduating to headlining slots at the flagship. "You used to have to leave for Nashville or Austin to find that kind of progression. Now some of us don't have to leave at all."

Whitfield has also worked with Bossier City tourism officials to position the venues as a draw distinct from the casinos themselves, arguing that diversifying the city's entertainment identity benefits the broader local economy, including nearby restaurants and hotels that see spillover business on show nights.

Looking ahead, Whitfield is in early discussions about a fourth, larger venue that would allow Red River Rhythm to compete for mid-sized touring acts while preserving its commitment to regional artists on undercards and off-nights. "I want people flying into Shreveport-Bossier specifically because of what's happening on our stages," he said. "We're not there yet. But we're a lot closer than anybody thought we'd get."
