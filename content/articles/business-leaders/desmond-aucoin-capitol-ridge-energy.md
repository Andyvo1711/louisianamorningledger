When Desmond Aucoin left a stable engineering job at a large utility contractor to start Capitol Ridge Energy Solutions out of a rented warehouse bay off Airline Highway, colleagues told him he was trading a pension for a headache. Nine years later, Capitol Ridge employs 140 people, holds contracts with three parish governments, and has become one of the more closely watched mid-sized energy firms in the Baton Rouge corridor.

"I kept seeing the same problem on job sites," Aucoin said. "Aging substations, crews stretched thin, and nobody building the bridge between old infrastructure and where the grid actually needs to go. I figured somebody should build that bridge as a business, not just complain about it."

## Building a Company Around Grid Modernization

Capitol Ridge started as a conventional electrical contracting outfit, handling substation upgrades and transmission line maintenance for municipal utilities across the Florida Parishes. Aucoin used those early, unglamorous contracts to fund research into battery storage integration, an area he'd studied but never had the capital to pursue at his previous job.

By 2022, Capitol Ridge had completed its first utility-scale battery storage pilot, a modest two-megawatt installation supporting a rural cooperative outside Denham Springs. The project ran over budget and behind schedule, lessons Aucoin now discusses openly at industry conferences. "We nearly sank the company on that first battery job," he said. "But it taught us more than ten years of substation work ever could."

That hard-won expertise positioned Capitol Ridge to win larger contracts as Louisiana utilities began seriously investing in storage to manage demand spikes during summer heat waves. The company completed a twelve-megawatt storage installation near Gonzales in 2025 and is currently under contract for a similar project serving industrial customers along the Mississippi River corridor.

## A Workforce Pipeline Rooted in Baton Rouge

Aucoin has been deliberate about hiring locally, partnering with Baton Rouge Community College to create an apprenticeship track specifically for battery and grid technology, distinct from the school's traditional electrical lineman program. Graduates split time between classroom instruction and paid fieldwork on active Capitol Ridge projects.

"Desmond doesn't just hire graduates, he helped design the curriculum they graduate from," said Renata Sowell, the BRCC program coordinator who worked with Aucoin to launch the track in 2023. "That kind of employer investment is rare, and it means our students are trained on the exact equipment they'll use on day one."

## Looking Toward Industrial Demand

With data centers and advanced manufacturing facilities increasingly eyeing sites along the I-10 and I-12 corridors, Aucoin sees Capitol Ridge's next chapter in serving industrial customers who need reliable, flexible power well beyond what the existing grid comfortably supports. The company recently opened a second facility in Gonzales specifically to house engineering staff focused on industrial-scale projects.

"Every one of these prospective employers asks the same question first: can the power keep up," Aucoin said. "That's not a hypothetical for us anymore, that's the whole business."

Aucoin remains hands-on, often visiting job sites in steel-toed boots rather than staying behind a desk in Capitol Ridge's Baton Rouge headquarters. He credits that habit with keeping the company's growth grounded. "The day I stop showing up on-site is the day this company starts making decisions that sound good in a boardroom and fall apart in the field," he said. "I've watched that happen to other outfits. Not going to happen here."
