Marguerite Fontenot still remembers the first time she unlocked the door of a shuttered nine-room guesthouse on Dauphine Street, mop bucket in one hand and a stack of unpaid utility notices in the other. Fourteen years later, that same building anchors what has become Bayou Crown Hospitality, a five-property boutique hotel company that now employs more than 220 people across New Orleans.

"I didn't set out to build a company," Fontenot said from the sunlit courtyard of her flagship property, the Crown & Crescent Inn. "I set out to keep the lights on and treat guests the way my grandmother treated visitors to her house on Esplanade. Everything else grew from that."

## From One Guesthouse to a Neighborhood Footprint

Bayou Crown's expansion has been deliberate rather than explosive. After stabilizing the Crown & Crescent Inn in her first three years, Fontenot acquired a struggling motor court in Bywater and converted it into the Ferryman's Rest, a property that leans into the neighborhood's arts scene with rotating exhibits from local painters and muralists. She followed with two more conversions in Mid-City and a fifth property near Freret Street that caters specifically to traveling nurses and academics visiting Tulane and Loyola.

Each property keeps its own identity rather than adopting a uniform corporate look, a strategy Fontenot credits to her background as an interior designer before she ever ran a business. "Travelers can smell a formula from a mile away," she said. "They want to feel like they stumbled onto something that belongs only to that block."

The company weathered the pandemic-era collapse in tourism by pivoting two of its five properties to extended-stay housing for traveling healthcare workers and utility crews, a decision that kept occupancy above 60 percent when many competitors closed entirely. That flexibility, Fontenot says, is now baked into how Bayou Crown plans for the future.

## Betting on Workforce Before Buildings

Unlike many hospitality operators who measure growth purely in room counts, Fontenot has invested heavily in staff development. Bayou Crown runs an in-house training program that moves housekeeping staff into front-desk and eventually property-management roles, and the company covers ServSafe and hospitality-management certification costs for any employee who completes a year of service.

"Our turnover is a fraction of the industry average because people can see a path here," said Devon Achee, Bayou Crown's director of operations, who himself started as a night auditor at the Crown & Crescent Inn eight years ago. "Marguerite treats staff development like it's capital investment, because it is."

That approach has paid dividends as New Orleans tourism has rebounded strongly heading into 2026, with convention bookings and Mardi Gras season visitation both running ahead of pre-pandemic benchmarks. Bayou Crown's occupancy across all five properties averaged above 78 percent in the fourth quarter of 2025, and Fontenot says she is now scouting a sixth location, likely in the Marigny.

Fontenot has also become a visible advocate for independent hoteliers navigating short-term rental regulations, testifying before the New Orleans City Council on zoning rules she says should distinguish between absentee investors and locally rooted operators like herself. "I live eleven blocks from my first hotel," she told council members last fall. "That's not incidental. That's the whole business model."

As Bayou Crown eyes further growth, Fontenot insists the company will keep its footprint concentrated in New Orleans rather than expanding into other Louisiana cities. "There are hospitality groups that want to be everywhere," she said. "I want to be the best version of here."
